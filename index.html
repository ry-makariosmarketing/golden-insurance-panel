Did one of these arrive?

Two notices that mean your panel is the problem

California carriers have ramped this up sharply in the last few months. If you got either of these, the clock is already running.

A non-renewal letter

Your carrier says they will not renew your homeowners policy unless the panel is replaced, usually with a hard deadline attached.

An inspection photo request

At renewal the insurer asks you to photograph your panel inside and out. Those photos decide whether you stay covered, get surcharged, or get dropped.

Why is this suddenly happening?

Most homeowners have no idea why they got the letter

You did nothing wrong. California insurers have changed what they will cover, and older panels are at the top of the list.

01

Some panels are now "uninsurable"

Carriers have decided specific panel brands and older wiring are fire hazards they will no longer take on.

02

Age is a trigger

Per insurance brokers, anything roughly 25 years or older is being called out for replacement.

03

Old wiring counts too

Knob-and-tube and other dated wiring draws the same flag as the panel itself.

04

It is accelerating

This has built for about two years and spiked in the last few months. Golden fields these calls daily.

Questions

Insurance panel replacement FAQ

How do I know if my panel is one of the flagged ones?
Check the brand name on the panel label, or send us a photo. Brands like Federal Pacific, Zinsco, Challenger, and Pushmatic are commonly flagged, but your carrier's letter lists the exact ones. We confirm it free during the inspection.
What happens if I miss my insurer's deadline?
You risk non-renewal, meaning your policy is dropped, or a much higher premium to stay covered. Replacing the panel on time is almost always cheaper than losing coverage or paying the surcharge year after year.
Does replacing the panel actually keep my coverage?
In the cases we see, yes. Once the flagged panel is replaced to current standard and documented, carriers restore or continue coverage. We give you the paperwork and photos your insurer asks for.
Can you really do it within 30 to 60 days?
Yes. We prioritize insurance-deadline jobs. Book the inspection as soon as your letter arrives and we will schedule the replacement inside your window.
My whole neighborhood got letters. Can you help all of us?
That is exactly what our HOA and neighborhood bundling is for. Group the replacements for priority scheduling and better per-home pricing, with one point of contact for the board.
